Rules for the repo

  • Commit every study day. The green squares on your GitHub profile prove you're consistent.
  • Write-ups in your own words. Explain the why, not only the payload. Recruiters read these.
  • Never publish real bug details from bug bounty programs until they're fixed and the program allows disclosure. Real findings go in bug-bounty/private/, which git ignores.
  • The tools are for you to build. Each folder in tools/ has a spec. Writing them yourself is the exercise.
  • Your vulnerable app runs on localhost only. Never deploy it publicly.

Rules so you don't quit again

  1. Never miss two days in a row. One bad day is life. Two is the start of quitting.
  2. Labs, not videos. You quit Wi-Fi hacking and Burp while watching. You won't quit while breaking things.
  3. One resource at a time. PortSwigger is the main track. Don't add a new course until this one is finished.
  4. Stuck for 30 minutes? Read the solution, understand it, then redo the lab without looking. Ego kills momentum.
  5. Finish proves the doubt wrong. Every checked box and every write-up is evidence. Doubt comes from unfinished things.
  6. Protect your VU degree. It's your ticket to the Google internship. Attendance first.
  7. Consistency beats intensity. 5 focused hours every day beats 14 hours for a week and then burnout.
  8. Learn in public. Post a weekly progress update on LinkedIn or X.

⚖️ The legal line

Only test systems you own or have written permission to test: labs, CTFs, your own apps, and in-scope bug bounty targets. Hacking without permission is a crime and can end a security career before it starts.
